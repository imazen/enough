# how-far measurements — 2026-10-06

Source: commit `2bcc73a` (working tree identical), and `cb49a7f5` for the
overhead matrix. Host: `r5900xt`, AMD Ryzen 9 5900XT (Zen 3), 32 threads,
native Ubuntu (Linux 7.0). Instruction counts come from `perf stat -e instructions:u`
and do not depend on machine load; cycles depend on it far less than wall time.
This record replaces the dated records of earlier designs, which remain in the
git history.

## Overhead by observer and library scenario

`python3 dev/how-far-checkpoint-cost/measure.py` at `cb49a7f5`. Each cell
runs the same `#[inline(never)]` PNG Sub defilter over a 256 KiB buffer and
is compared with the same work without how-far, serially or on four scoped
threads. Values are medians of five interleaved runs. Plans (`Stages`,
fork-join) build a fresh observer per operation, as tracking one operation
would; the other scenarios reuse one observer. The parallel columns vary by
about ±3,000 instructions per buffer from thread spawn and join, which is why
some cells are negative. Cycles also vary with machine load, so compare them
only within one run.

Per checkpoint, at 256 checkpoints per buffer:
- `step` into a `PulseTree` costs 65 to 77 instructions, depending on its stop policy;
- `StopOnly` costs 28;
- an `FnPulse` callback costs 106;
- `WithStop` costs about 40 more than the tree it wraps;
- `&NoPulse` costs nothing with `step`, and one call with a bare `check`.

Pacing brings every observer except `DiagnosticPulse` under 0.3%.

`DiagnosticPulse` reads the clock for each report and at the start of each
check, and at the end of a sampled check (see `Stats::check_time`). It also
locks the span on every call. So it is for measuring, paced or not.

### Changes since `2bcc73a`

Instructions are deterministic, so these compare directly with the previous
record of this table:
- **Tracked plans.** 3 `Stages` with a `PulseTree` went from 8,535 to 7,666
  extra instructions per operation. Phase names and units are stored inline,
  and a split builds its children without an intermediate `Vec`. DHAT counts
  31 → 21 allocations for this operation; a fork-join of four goes 38 → 32.
- **`DiagnosticPulse`.**
  - It times only a sample of checks and keeps its span state in
    nanoseconds.
  - It tries the previous call site first.
  - `step` costs 228,501 extra instructions per buffer, down from 341,719.

Cycles for `DiagnosticPulse`, old binary (`9b23910`) against new
(`cb49a7f5`), as medians of seven interleaved runs in one session:

| Scenario | `9b23910` | `cb49a7f5` |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `check` / chunk | +96.9% | +56.4% |
| `step` / chunk | +169.3% | +126.8% |
| 4 workers, `step` (one shared span) | +209.6% | +188.2% |

The counter now uses one `fetch_add` per report, saturating at 2^63 − 1.
Single-threaded it costs the same instructions as the compare-and-swap loop
it replaces, and at 1 KiB per step the two measure the same. At 16 bytes per
step with 4 workers on one phase it halves the cycles, median 5.13M → 2.74M
per buffer, because a compare-and-swap loop retries under contention.

## enough: stop policies by check pattern

`python3 dev/how-far-checkpoint-cost/measure.py stop`: the same defilter and
method, with each `enough` / `almost-enough` / `enough-tokio` stop policy
checked six ways and compared with the loop without checks. `TokioStop` wraps
a tokio-util `CancellationToken`; `PollMeter` is the opt-in poll-latency
instrument; `WithTimeout` and `DebouncedTimeout` wrap a `Stopper` with an
hour-long deadline, so they read the clock but never fire.

Per check: `Stopper`, `SyncStopper` and `StopRef` cost about 10 instructions
through `&dyn Stop` and about 2 or fewer when the loop is generic over the
policy. On x86-64 an Acquire load is an ordinary load, so `SyncStopper` costs
the same as `Stopper`; on Neoverse-N1 it costs one more instruction per check
through `&dyn`, with no cycle difference this measurement can resolve.
`Unstoppable` through `&dyn Stop` is one call per check (about 6 instructions);
a generic loop, or `may_stop()` asked once before the loop, removes it.
`WithTimeout` reads the clock on every check (about 112 instructions, +22%
cycles on x86-64 at this granularity); `DebouncedTimeout` brings that to about
23 instructions, with its cycle cost within this measurement's noise. `ChildStopper` walks its
parents (about 36 instructions at depth 2) whether or not the loop is generic,
and `TokioStop` costs about 47. `PollMeter` is for measuring, at about 690.
Checking every 16th chunk instead of every chunk makes every policy except
`PollMeter` cost under 1%.

## Real encoder adoption

`python3 dev/adopt-zenpng.py ~/work/zen/zenpng <dir>` materializes zenpng
`27393eef` with feature-gated progress, then
`cargo test --manifest-path <dir>/probe/Cargo.toml --features progress`:
three tests pass (byte-identical serial and parallel output with tracked
strategy counts, cancellation after real encoder work, and the old
cancellation API preserved).

Not measured here: wall-time overhead (zenbench), cold-build wall time, and the
zenresize and zenav1-svt integrations, which still use earlier APIs.
